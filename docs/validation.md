# Validation of the SkillAlign method

Generated by tools/validate.py over 20 random samples of the synthetic generator, whose true parameters are known.

## 1. False alarms on data with no skill trends

| Trend-free data | First version (growth thresholds only) | Now (role-stratified test, Bonferroni) | Review/Prepare advice now, all courses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fully flat | 1.2 per sample (max 3) | 0.1 per sample (max 1) | 0.1 per sample (max 1) |
| Only the mix of job roles changes | 4.7 per sample (max 8) | 0.0 per sample (max 0) | 0.1 per sample (max 1) |
| Flat, with employers reposting ads | 1.4 per sample (max 3) | 0.1 per sample (max 1) | 0.1 per sample (max 1) |

## 2. Detection on trended data

Truth is the tool's own rule applied to the generator's TRUE values (market-wide +30% or -20%, with a within-role change of at least half that, in 5%+ of postings): 7 of 26 reportable skills qualify, so a random guesser would be right 27% of the time.

- Precision: 33 of 55 reported trends meet that rule in truth (60%); 54 of 55 (98%) point the right way on a real within-role change of 10% or more. 2.8 trends are reported per sample.
- Recall: 33 of 140 true trends found across samples (24%).

| Size of the true within-role change (true trends only) | Found |
|---|---|
| 15-50% | 13 of 120 (11%) |
| 50-100% | 0 of 0 (0%) |
| over 100% | 20 of 20 (100%) |

The test is deliberately cautious: it finds every large change but misses most modest ones at this sample size (about 1,100 postings a window), and what it reports almost always points the right way.

## 4. Forecast backtest

Fitted on the first six quarters and asked for the eighth, for every skill in at least 5% of postings: mean absolute error 1.6 percentage points; the 95% prediction range contained the actual share 91% of the time (394 forecasts).
