        How it works

        Every number on this site is computed by a small Python program that runs in your browser. There is no AI model and no server: the same data always gives the same answer.

        1. Reading skills out of text

        A taxonomy lists 60 skills, each with the different ways people write it ("AWS", "Azure" and "cloud platforms" all mean Cloud Computing). All the phrases are joined into one regular expression, longest first, so "cloud security" is counted as Cloud Security and not also as Cloud Computing. The same reader is used on job postings, syllabi and resumes.

        2. Measuring demand

        A skill's share is the fraction of postings that mention it. We compare the latest four quarters with the four before them to get growth, and fit a straight line through the quarterly shares (least squares) to forecast the share four quarters ahead.

        3. Scoring a program

        Only postings for the program's own target roles count. Skills in at least 15% of those postings are core. The alignment score is the share-weighted part of core demand the program teaches, so missing a skill that 60% of jobs want costs far more than missing one that 16% want. The future score repeats this with forecast shares. Hours on demand is the share of training hours spent on core skills.

        4. Recommendations

        • Add: a core skill the program does not teach, ranked by share boosted by growth.
        • Expand: taught, and asked for in 40% or more of postings, but given under 5% of the training hours.
        • Prepare: not core yet, but forecast to cross 15% within a year.
        • Reduce: taught, but asked for in under 5% of postings.
        • Review: taught and still asked for, but down 20% or more year on year.

        Limits

        Postings say what employers advertise, not everything a job needs. Skill names outside the taxonomy are not detected. With fewer than 30 matching postings a score is only a rough signal, and the page says so. The built-in data is synthetic; upload real postings for real conclusions.

        SkillAlign is a student prototype for the Smart India Hackathon problem "Challenges in aligning skill development programs with industry requirements and emerging job market demands". Built in Python, run in the browser with Pyodide.
